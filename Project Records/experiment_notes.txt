Cooja Experiment 1: DIS Flooding Attack in RPL/6LoWPAN

Purpose:
To recreate a DIS flooding style RPL attack in Contiki-NG/Cooja and compare its network activity against a normal RPL UDP baseline.

Baseline setup:
Simulation: baseline_rpl_udp_16_motes
Motes: 16
Attacker: none
Run time: 05:01.462
Radio packets: 4660

Attack setup:
Simulation: attack_dis_flood_17_motes
Motes: 17
Attacker: mote 17
Attack type: DIS flooding
Run time: 05:00.883
Radio packets: 5004
Attack log evidence:
ID:17 [INFO: DIS-FLOODER] ATTACK: sending multicast DIS

Result:
The DIS flooding run produced 5004 radio packets compared with 4660 packets in the normal baseline. This is an increase of 344 packets, around 7% more radio activity over roughly 5 minutes.

Interpretation:
The malicious mote repeatedly sent multicast DIS messages. This increased radio activity compared with the normal RPL UDP baseline. This supports the idea that DIS flooding creates extra routing/control overhead in a 6LoWPAN/RPL network.

Connection to Gope et al. paper:
Gope et al. classify DIS flooding as a direct-resource-topology RPL attack. This Cooja experiment recreates a small-scale version of that attack and observes its impact on radio traffic.


Fair DIS Flooding Comparison

Control simulation:
File: control silent 16 motes.csc
Setup: 1 server, 14 normal clients, 1 silent mote
Time: 05:00.747
Radio packets: 4143

DIS attack simulation:
File: attack_dis_flood_16_motes.csc
Setup: 1 server, 14 normal clients, 1 DIS flooding attacker
Attacker: mote 17
Time: 05:00.900
Radio packets: 4509

Result:
The DIS flooding simulation produced 4509 packets compared with 4143 packets in the silent control simulation.

Difference:
4509 - 4143 = 366 extra packets

Interpretation:
This is a fairer comparison because both simulations used 16 total motes. The only main difference was that the silent mote was replaced with a DIS flooding attacker. This suggests that the DIS flooding attacker increased radio activity by repeatedly sending multicast DIS messages.



Blackhole Attack Experiment: Forced Three-Mote Path

Topology:
Mote 1: UDP server and RPL root, position (20, 50)
Mote 2: Intermediate router, position (55, 50)
Mote 3: Victim UDP client, position (90, 50)

Radio configuration:
Transmission range: 50 metres
Interference range: 100 metres

The 70-metre distance between motes 1 and 3 prevented direct
communication. Mote 3 therefore had to communicate through mote 2.

Blackhole attack:
The network was allowed to converge normally for 90 seconds.
Before activation, requests 0 to 4 all received responses.
At 01:30.236, mote 2 enabled the blackhole behaviour.

Measurement window: 01:30 to 06:30
Requests sent by victim: 30
Responses received: 0
Delivery rate: 0%
Packet loss: 100%

Control:
The same topology, radio configuration and measurement window were
used, but mote 2 remained a normal forwarding router.

Requests sent by victim: 30
Responses received: 30
Delivery rate: 100%
Packet loss: 0%

Result:
The control router successfully forwarded every measured request and
response. After blackhole activation, the victim continued transmitting
requests, but received no responses. This demonstrates that the
blackhole router suppressed forwarded traffic and caused complete
application-level packet loss in the measured five-minute period.
